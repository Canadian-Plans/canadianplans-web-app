# Canadian Plans — business growth research

Research date: 22 October 2026 (Asia/Dhaka). Purpose: inform the owner's business interview and a later ChatGPT project prompt. This is external evidence and proposed reasoning, not an approved strategy, sales forecast, or confirmation of launch readiness.

## Scope and business assumptions

Canadian Plans is developing an advisor-assisted telecom sales business with activation-based provider commissions and potential referral-partner commissions. The research considers BYOD mobile, pre-arrival eSIM, and province-specific internet/TV. Actual sellable offers, authorization, margins, launch scope, customer support capacity, and goals require owner confirmation. Latest owner decisions take precedence over older repository planning; the external sources below do not establish Canadian Plans' carrier authorization or operating status.

Existing marketing notes live in this directory. This report adds research only; it does not amend architecture, offers, policies, or implementation.

### Owner interview update — 22 October 2026

The owner reports no Canadian Plans sales yet, a primary audience preparing to move to Canada, and an organic SEO/marketing strategy with no paid ads. The owner also reports a separate similar pre-arrival SIM business receiving roughly 30 orders monthly from organic SEO and asks for realistic proposed goals. This is self-reported context, not verified analytics. Orders are not necessarily activations or realized commissions. The separate business is not Canadian Plans, and its customer data, brand, rankings, content, or authority must not be assumed transferable.

**Revised research priority:** evaluate organic acquisition for pre-arrival buyers first. Establish source countries, destination mix, existing search demand and competition, and the owner's accessible distribution before proposing a numerical goal. Request aggregated prior-site data: date range, organic sessions/clicks, landing pages and queries, submitted orders, successful activations, conversion lags, and contribution per activation. The prior business's 30 monthly orders can inform a scenario only after identifying differences in domain maturity, offers, traffic, operating effort, and migration overlap. No forecast has been derived from it here.

Further country-specific competitor research, keyword prioritization, channel goals, and realistic time-to-result scenarios depend on those owner answers. A sensible initial goal structure is verified measurement and published useful service content, followed by observed qualified enquiries and activations; numeric milestones should be proposed with explicit assumptions and revised against actual results.

### 1. Newcomer demand exists, but student growth cannot be assumed

IRCC's 2026–2028 plan sets 2026 targets of 155,000 new international student arrivals and 230,000 new temporary workers, alongside 380,000 permanent resident admissions. Temporary arrival targets exclude visitors, permit extensions, and in-Canada study/work applications. Permanent admissions include transitions of people already in Canada. These are policy targets, not realized customer counts; adding them together would overstate a simple pre-arrival market estimate. Targets for 2027–2028 remain adjustable.

**Implication (inference):** interview the owner about the intended mix of students, workers, other newcomers, and existing Canadian residents. Build acquisition assumptions from reachable audiences and confirmed offers, not a historical immigration growth narrative.

**Source/date:** [IRCC supplementary 2026–2028 levels plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/immigration-system/levels/supplementary-immigration-levels-2026-2028.html), accessed 22 October 2026. Reference period is 2026–2028; the retrieved page did not expose a publication date.

### 2. Population stocks are different from new arrivals

Statistics Canada's indexed official September 2026 release reports 2,779,774 non-permanent residents on 1 July 2026, down 154,614 from a year earlier. This is a population stock, not annual arrivals, unique telecom buyers, or an addressable sales funnel.

**Access limitation:** the official search result exposed these figures, but direct HTML/PDF retrieval returned availability errors. Treat them as provisional research context until the release can be reopened; do not put them in external marketing copy yet.

**Implication (inference):** keep existing-resident and pre-arrival demand separate, and refresh demographic evidence before any market-sizing exercise.

**Source/date:** [Statistics Canada, age and gender estimates 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260923/dq260923a-eng.htm), released 23 September 2026; reference date 1 July 2026. [Official immigration statistics portal](https://www.statcan.gc.ca/en/subjects-start/immigration_and_ethnocultural_diversity/immigrants_and_nonpermanent_residents) also indexed the stock figure.

### 3. Competition is substantial; generic cheap-plan positioning is weak

The CRTC reports that Canada's three largest mobile operators held 90% of the market in 2024, while new competitors accounted for more than 55% of subscribers added that year. It describes slowing wireless growth and improved perceptions of choice. These are operator-level results, not the market share or economics of sales intermediaries.

**Implication (inference):** test a specific reason to choose Canadian Plans: access through trusted communities, a simpler application, suitable verified offers, or responsive assistance. Each needs evidence. Do not infer dealer margins, acquisition costs, or Canadian Plans' savings from national operator statistics.

**Source/date:** [CRTC Canadian Telecommunications Market Report 2026](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/publications/reports/PolicyMonitoring/2026/ctmr.htm), page modified 24 February 2026; cited market-share figures describe 2024, not October 2026.

### 4. Pre-arrival connectivity is an existing competitive offer

Rogers' newcomer page promotes reserving a SIM and Canadian number before arrival and links directly to CanadianSIM as its partner. It also presents mobile and home services. This verifies an established competing customer proposition; it does not establish Canadian Plans' relationship with either organization.

**Implication (inference):** pre-arrival convenience alone is not demonstrated differentiation. Ask what the owner can deliver or distribute better for a specific customer group, and what proof supports that claim. Use this as one comparator, not a complete competitor census.

**Source/date:** [Rogers newcomer services](https://www.rogers.com/newcomers), live page accessed 22 October 2026; no publication date shown. Recheck offers and relationships before quoting externally.

### 5. SEO should answer real purchase questions with original evidence

Google recommends content with original value, clear sourcing, demonstrable expertise, and a useful experience for the intended audience. It cautions against mass-producing many topics, merely rewriting other sources, artificial freshness, and content made primarily to manipulate rankings.

**Implication (inference):** prioritize verified service pages and genuine customer questions: device eligibility, activation timing, what is payable and when, documents actually required, porting, and address-specific internet availability. These are candidate content themes, not validated keyword demand. Publish location pages only where they add useful, accurate local information.

**Source/date:** [Google people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), last updated 5 October 2026; operational guidance, not market data.

### 6. AI search does not justify speculative special markup

Google's AI-features page says normal SEO practices remain relevant. Supporting pages must be indexed and eligible for a search snippet; special AI text files or special schema are not required. Inclusion is not guaranteed. AI-feature traffic is included in overall Search Console Web performance reporting.

**Implication (inference):** judge SEO by qualified enquiries and eventual activations as well as visibility. Avoid promising AI citations or making unverified AI-traffic attribution claims. Keep important offer information available in readable text and consistent with structured data.

**Source/date:** [Google AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features), last updated 10 December 2025; accessed 22 October 2026.

### 7. Google Business Profile eligibility must be established

Google generally requires in-person contact during stated business hours. Its ineligible categories explicitly include online-only businesses and lead-generation agents or companies.

**Implication (inference):** do not automatically recommend Maps listings or invented Canadian offices for an online intermediary. Confirm the actual operating model and in-person customer service before considering a Business Profile. Organic pages for genuinely served regions are a separate strategy.

**Source/date:** [Google Business Profile eligibility](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036?hl=en), accessed 22 October 2026; no update date shown.

### 8. Outreach needs a defined consent basis

The CRTC summarizes commercial email/text requirements as consent, identification/contact details, and a working unsubscribe mechanism; it also notes statutory exceptions and exemptions. A contact obtained through an agency or community is not, by that fact alone, proof of permission for every marketing use.

**Implication (inference):** distinguish permission to post in a community from permission to send direct marketing or add people to a mailing list. Define consent capture, ownership of partner leads, and opt-out handling before scaling campaigns. Evaluate exact message/channel facts when applying exceptions rather than assuming every business message is exempt.

**Source/date:** [CRTC CASL overview](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/internet/anti/reg.htm), modified 26 March 2026; general official guidance, not a determination about a particular campaign.

### 9. Advertised prices need complete conditions

The Competition Bureau says prices that cannot be attained because mandatory fixed non-government fees are added raise drip-pricing concerns; variable fees can also raise concerns.

**Implication (inference):** distinguish no payment when submitting an enquiry from free telecom service. Marketing should use current approved provider terms, including mandatory charges, promotion duration, eligibility, and later billing. Never generalize Canadian Plans' lack of a fee into a promise that the provider charges none.

**Source/date:** [Competition Bureau drip-pricing guidance](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/drip-pricing), modified 24 August 2026.
