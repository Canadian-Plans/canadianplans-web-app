# Canadian Plans — SEO content research and rewrite notes

Research date: 23 October 2026. Purpose: ground the storefront copy rewrite on this branch in
what competitors publish, what newcomers actually search for, and what the owner has confirmed.
Everything here is observation and reasoning, not a sales forecast. No keyword volumes are
quoted: the Semrush connection had no API units left on the day, so demand is ranked by intent
and by what competing pages target, not by measured volume. Re-run keyword volume checks in
Search Console once the site is indexed.

## 2. Who ranks today, and how they sell

Observed on 23 October 2026 from the public pages. Claims below are theirs, not verified.

### Get Canada SIM (getcanadasim.com) — the owner's former business, now a competitor

- Title: "Canada SIM Card & eSIM | Ready Before You Land". H1: "Get Your Canadian SIM Before
  You Land". Hook: "plans from $1/day", roaming at "$12–15/day" versus eSIM at about
  "$1.50/day".
- Same Rogers/Fido price points as ours ($35/75 GB, $40/100 GB, $50/120 GB), with crossed-out
  "in Canada" prices ($60, $80) we have chosen not to repeat.
- Sells "pay 20 days after activation", "no credit history, SIN or local ID", "support in
  10+ languages", a physical-SIM option, segment pages (newcomers, students, workers,
  tourists), a blog and a partner programme. Trustpilot 3.5.
- Weak spot we can use: generic segment copy, little explanation of eligibility (who gets
  postpaid, who gets prepaid), no province-level internet content.

### CanadianSIM (canadiansim.com) — Rogers' official pre-arrival partner

- Title: "Canadian SIM For Newcomers – Affordable Pre-Arrival Plans". H1 "Get your Canadian
  SIM, before you fly". "Trusted by 200,000+ newcomers", "4.9 Google rating", "125+ partners"
  (IDP and others). Linked from rogers.com/newcomers.
- Same price points; takes a deposit at checkout that is credited to the first bill; number
  available up to 90 days before arrival; data-usage alerts; phone + plan bundles.
- Student page targets "SIM card for international students", with an arrival-date planner
  and FAQ ("What is the best SIM card for students in Canada?", "How much does a student SIM
  cost?", "Can I get a Canadian phone number before arriving?").
- Weak spot: thin long-form content; every page funnels to checkout with a deposit, which we
  can contrast honestly ("nothing to pay online").

### PhoneBox, welcomeaide, moving2canada, canamigrate, IDP

- PhoneBox sells to students through university and IDP partnerships: "no credit history, no
  activation fee, no contract".
- The informational results for "best phone plan for international students Canada",

  (welcomeaide), immigration portals (moving2canada, canamigrate) and IDP. They rank on
  topic depth: carrier comparison tables, documents required, number porting, calling home,
  credit checks. None of them sells a specific pre-arrival plan with a clear price.

### Carriers

- rogers.com/newcomers: "Welcome to Canada, welcome to Rogers", eSIM and number before
  arrival via CanadianSIM, 100 languages, 200 locations. No prices on the page.

## 3. Weak points in the current storefront copy

1. **Page titles are labels, not search phrases.** "Home Internet | Canadian Plans",
   "Mobile Plans | Canadian Plans", "Pre-arrival SIM & eSIM | Canadian Plans". None says
   "Canada", "newcomer", "before you arrive", "no credit check" or a price.
2. **Meta descriptions are placeholders** ("Explore Canadian mobile plans and newcomer
   connectivity with Canadian Plans").
3. **Trust signals were invented** (reviews, response time, WhatsApp, a fictional advisor).
   Competitors have real ones. The honest substitutes are: authorised reseller, nothing paid
   online, a named advisor confirms every order, a Canadian address, published hours and a
   plain explanation of who the contract is with.
4. **Jargon.** "Pre-arrival" is our term, not the customer's. Searches say "before I arrive",
   "before you land", "SIM card for new immigrants", "phone plan for work permit holders".
5. **Audience gaps.** Competitors have pages for workers, tourists and permanent residents;
   we only had students, India and Toronto Pearson. Visitors (chatr prepaid, Super Visa) are
   a distinct product with distinct queries and had no page.
6. **One real guide.** Informational queries are won by depth. The index listed landing
   pages as guides to fill space.
7. **Structured data gaps.** FAQ and breadcrumb JSON-LD existed on landing pages and the FAQ
   page but not on the three category pages, and there was no Organization schema.
8. **Pending answer slots** ("This answer is awaiting approved content") were shown on live
   FAQ questions, including ones the owner has already answered (cancellation, what happens
   when data runs out, activation timing).

## 5. Rules applied in the rewrite

- Every claim traces to `LAUNCH_PLANS.md`, `OPEN_INPUTS.md` or the static catalogue. Where
  the owner has not decided (student discount, languages, parents paying), the FAQ item keeps
  its visible pending slot and stays out of JSON-LD.
- Prices appear with "CAD, plus tax" and the date they were last checked. No competitor
  prices, no "regular price" strike-throughs.
- "Nothing paid online" is never written as "free service". The provider bills after
  activation and collects its own taxes and fees.
- Titles under about 60 characters where possible, descriptions 120–160 characters, one H1
  per page carrying the primary phrase, a quick-answer H2 phrased as the question people
  type, FAQ answers that stand alone when quoted.
- Plain English: short sentences, "you" and "we", no marketing filler.

## 7. Second pass, 23 October 2026: keyword and audience research for the guides

Semrush still had no API units on this pass, so demand is again judged from what competing
pages target and from the questions that recur in forums, settlement guides and vendor FAQs.

### Who the audience is

- IRCC admissions reported through mid-2026 put **India** first for both permanent residents
  (about a quarter of admissions) and study permits, with the **Philippines**, **China**,
  **Nigeria** and **Bangladesh** the other large sources, and **Pakistan** and **Cameroon**
  close behind for permanent residence. Working-holiday (IEC) arrivals from the UK, Ireland,
  Australia and France are a smaller, English-speaking audience with their own forums.
- Across every source country the same three pains recur: a Canadian number before the bank
  appointment, a phone that may be locked or missing bands, and one-time passwords from home
  (UPI, GCash, bank apps, Aadhaar, BVN) that still go to the old number.

### What competitors already publish

- Get Canada SIM's blog has about 50 posts from July to October 2026 covering plan choice,
  eSIM setup, IMEI/EID, unlocking, dual SIM, porting, area codes and coverage. CanadianSIM
  lists 142 guides, heavy on per-device pages (iPhone 14 to 16, Galaxy S23/S24, Pixel 8),
  deposits and billing. Both are thin on immigration-status eligibility, on the order of
  first-week admin, and on keeping a home number alive for OTPs.
- Settlement sites (welcomeaide, prepareforcanada, MOSAIC) rank for "first week in Canada"
  and "newcomer checklist" with day-by-day lists that mention the phone number but not how
  to have it before landing.

### Facts checked for these guides

- SIN documents and application routes: canada.ca SIN application page, 23 October 2026.
- Rogers network bands: Rogers device compatibility page (LTE B2, B4, B5, B7, B12, B25, B66;
  5G n41, n66, n71) and Rogers community guidance on imported phones and VoLTE.
- GCash Overseas accepts a Canadian number for registration; loans and credit need a
  Philippine number (GCash Help Centre).
- Jio and Airtel OTP delivery abroad depends on roaming being enabled before departure
  (carrier guidance and user reports; worded as such in the guide).
- Provincial health waiting periods vary; the guide says "up to about three months" and tells
  readers to keep interim insurance rather than quoting one province's rule.
